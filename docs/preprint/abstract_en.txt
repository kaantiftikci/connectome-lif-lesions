Whole-brain connectomes of Drosophila now exist for both sexes, and leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) models built on them predict sensorimotor circuits with high accuracy. Whether such models can also simulate dopamine-dependent phenomena, the basis of fly models of learning and of Parkinson's disease, has not been tested. A cell-type-resolved lesion framework was built on the FlyWire female connectome (138,639 neurons) and the MaleCNS male connectome (164,606 neurons), the simulator was validated against the reference model (per-neuron rate correlation 0.963), and the effect of progressive loss of dopaminergic clusters (PAM, PPL1, PPL2, PPM1) on network output was measured under a six-stimulus battery, in an embodied body model, and through an amortised inverse model that infers lesions from phenotypes. In the model's default regime, loss of the 16 PPL1 neurons shifts the phenotype six times more than loss of 16 random central neurons, through three labelled lines (PPL105→MBON13, PPL103→MBON31, PAM08→MBON29) that switch mushroom-body output neurons off one at a time. This specificity, however, is an artefact: every odour input tested drives the model into a brain-wide attractor in which 75 % of Kenyon cells fire and output neurons saturate at 160 Hz. Restoring sparse Kenyon-cell activity (3.8 %) by strengthening APL inhibition abolishes every dopaminergic lesion effect: an inverse model trained on 1,000 random lesions of 32 dopaminergic subtypes recovers six subtypes in the attractor regime and none in the sparse regime (surrogate R² 0.52 against −0.02), and progressive loss of whole clusters stays within the noise floor (PPL1 distance 1.74 against a noise floor of 1.91, random loss of 307 neurons 1.84), with or without a dopamine-as-modulator layer fitted to conditioning data, and at synaptic weights 27 % below and above the reference (at the stronger weight the effect returns only as the network re-approaches the attractor). The reason is structural-dynamical: natural reinforcers (sugar, bitter, water) and mechanosensory inputs never activate PAM or PPL1 neurons (0 Hz), although sugar gustatory neurons reach 69 PAM neurons within two synapses. The only drive to dopaminergic neurons in the model is the odour attractor. Tracing the pathways shows why: the neurons that sugar activates send at most 1.9 mV to any PAM-projecting relay, no strengthening of that route up to 16-fold makes PAM fire, and the octopaminergic neurons that carry sugar reward in vivo receive no sugar input and barely contact PAM, whereas bitter taste reaches PPL1 through 20 identifiable relay neurons that fire once their input and output synapses are strengthened 4-fold, a testable prediction of where the bottleneck lies. Re-running the neuron-loss protocol of a recent amyloid-pathology simulation (Jo, 2026) in both regimes reproduces its ordering (hub damage worst, antihub mildest) but shows that its whole-brain activity readout reverses sign when a single APL neuron is lost. Supplying the reward route as one extrasynaptic term turns the negative result into a test: every odour-responsive output neuron that behavioural work assigns to appetitive memory then learns from sugar, with a silent reserve that differs by compartment (learning intact after 50 % PAM loss, dependent on which neurons die at 75 %, gone at 90 %). The same model yields testable predictions for fly Parkinson models: the connectome's synaptic load ranks PPL1 neurons 12-fold above PAM, matching the clusters lost in parkin and PINK1 flies; the order of loss sets when a memory fails while locomotor and passive readouts never show the loss; memories stored before the loss are kept while new ones cannot be formed; and tonic dopamine replacement restores nothing but, if the gate does not adapt, corrupts plasticity specifically in the compartments that learn. It is concluded that connectome-constrained LIF models, as currently parameterised, can rank structural damage but cannot propagate reinforcement to dopaminergic neurons, and that one explicit extrasynaptic term is enough to make them a predictive tool for dopamine-dependent learning and its loss. All tools are released as open code.
